**Two rules that override the table:**

## 6. Coaching Principles (Carry These Forward)

1. **Perceived vs. actual effort mismatch is Dan's primary limiter.** His feel runs roughly **15 bpm hot**. HR data overrides self-assessment, every time. "It felt easy" is the signal to check the trace, not to advance.
2. **Week progression is cleared by data, not by feeling** — talk test + HR peaks in the target band. Say so explicitly when clearing him, so the standard stays visible.
3. **The back-half surge is the recurring failure mode**, even when early intervals are disciplined. It shows up as: final reps 1.5–2:00/mi faster, Zone 4/5 spikes, and Strava PR badges on easy days. A medal on an easy day means the easy day wasn't easy.
4. **External constraints beat willpower.** Proven governors, ranked by effectiveness: his wife's pace > altitude > treadmill belt speed > the Zone 3 HR buzz > his own intention. Build in a governor rather than asking for restraint.
5. **The slow jog IS the workout**, not a warmup for real running later. Building the aerobic base at low HR is what eventually allows fast running. Easy is the win condition, not a problem to fix. Don't let "another week" become "I'll nudge the belt up since 5.5 is easy now."
6. **Cadence lever:** if pace feels insultingly slow, pick the feet up quicker rather than reaching farther. ~150 bpm playlist or metronome. Fixes both the slog feeling and the 119 spm cadence.
7. **Injury rule:** legs and lungs are ahead of the tendons. Connective tissue doesn't send a "too fast" signal until it's already hurt. Any nagging → repeat the week.

### How Dan communicates
Casual, self-deprecating, uses emoji naturally. Frequently front-runs the criticism ("I know what you're going to tell me…"). Pushes back on constraints but accepts them when given clear physiological reasoning. **Self-corrects reliably when shown his own data** — HR traces are the single most effective coaching tool. Responds well to direct, data-grounded feedback delivered with warmth, and to getting credit for the half he earned before the half he didn't.
